# Research integrity and disclosure

- Data, model checks, software perturbations, source-document evidence and
  proposed planning practice are different evidence classes. A software test
  is not an observed permitting outcome; a source-version sample is not proof
  of field PV performance. The register does not grant development rights.
- Results are retrospective and include failure cases and the competitive LP
  baseline. No preregistration, independent market sample, full LCOH, new
  theorem or measured institutional benefit is claimed.
- Historical data files and raw archives are not edited. The tolerance repair
  rejects four prior records without replacing regions or concealing the
  obsolete version. Reproduction outputs go to an explicit ignored work area.
- AI assistance was provided through OpenAI Codex for design discussion,
  literature/document discovery and extraction, code, checks, figures and
  manuscript drafting/editing. It was not limited to language polishing. It
  generated no empirical energy observations or land polygons. Relevant model
  and source-document passages were checked; final author responsibility and
  source review remain necessary. No exact unavailable backend version or
  personal contribution has been invented.
- The study uses public regional/environmental/energy data, not human or animal
  participants. No ethical committee approval number is invented. The author
  must confirm authorship, affiliations, funding, competing interests and all
  manuscript declarations before submission; the release cannot certify these
  personal declarations.
- The GitHub repository is a versioned working dissemination route. An actual
  immutable Zenodo deposit and DOI is a separate author-controlled step. No
  DOI, public deposition date or journal acceptance is prefilled.
- Raw boundary data and project PDFs are excluded from public redistribution.
  Dataset-specific rights and attribution apply; publicly accessible does not
  automatically mean freely relicensable.

Software tests and release checks are reported separately from peer review.
CI tests check the released numerical inputs and code; they do not rerun all
expensive operational scenarios or recreate immutable historical GIS servers.
